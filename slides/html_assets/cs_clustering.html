Overview of common clustering algorithms and their main features.

K-means

  • Partitions data into K clusters minimizing within-cluster variance.
  • Assumes spherical, equally sized clusters.
  • Fast, scalable, but sensitive to outliers and initialization.
Key params: n_clusters, init, n_init, max_iter

DBSCAN

  • Density-based: forms clusters of closely packed points.
  • Identifies outliers as noise; no need to predefine K.
  • Can struggle with varying densities or high-dimensional data.
Key params: eps, min_samples, metric

HDBSCAN

  • Hierarchical extension of DBSCAN with adaptive density.
  • Automatically finds stable clusters and handles noise robustly.
  • Excellent for high-dimensional embeddings (e.g., SBERT, UMAP).
Key params: min_cluster_size, min_samples, cluster_selection_epsilon

BIRCH

  • Incremental clustering using a feature tree structure.
  • Handles large or streaming datasets efficiently.
  • Works best for roughly spherical clusters.
Key params: threshold, branching_factor, n_clusters
💡 Tip: Use K-means for simple clusters, DBSCAN/HDBSCAN for arbitrary shapes and noise, BIRCH for large-scale or online data.
